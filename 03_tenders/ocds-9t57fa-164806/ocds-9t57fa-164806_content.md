PROBLEM STATEMENT\n2.1 In 2019 the SDM adopted a Spatial Planning and Land Use Management Act, 16 of 2013\n(SPLUMA) compliant SDF with a 2030 vision. According to SPLUMA, the Sedibeng SDF was\nscheduled for reviewed in 2024. However, this review was delayed pending the promulgation\nof the Vaal River Regional Spatial Development Framework, 2025. Given the timelines for\nSDF review and adoption, it is now necessary to initiate the review process for the Sedibeng\nSDF.\n2.2 As a result of an ever-changing policy and legislative environment, there is a need to align to\nthe National SDF, 2023 (2050), the newly adopted Gauteng SDF, 2025 (2055) as well as the\nVaal River Regional SDF, 2025.\n\n2.3 The local municipalities within the district have or are in the process of developing forward\nplanning transformation tools which necessitates the need for the district to be proactive and\nalign to the spatial direction for growth and development in the region. This also necessitates\nthe need for mapping updates.\n2.4 A district SDF must be reviewed in accordance with a 5-year cycle aligned with the Integrated\nDevelopment Plan (IDP) of the district municipality. Furthermore, the district economic focus\nand strategic direction has shifted from north to south to focusing on the Vaal River as an\neconomic pivot for the Vaal region.\n2.5 The Spatial Planning section of the district lacks the capacity to conduct extensive review and\npublic participation that is involved in the compilation of a Municipal SDF. Additionally, there\nis a need for an increased external perspective on the drafting of spatial planning policies to\nascertain that the municipality keeps abreast with new ideas and innovations.\n3.BACKGROUND AND LOCATIONAL CONTEXT\n3.1 Section 12 of SPLUMA requires all spheres of government (National, Provincial and\nMunicipalities) to develop SDFs. Section 20 of SPLUMA, read together with Section 26 of the\nMunicipal Systems Act, 32 of 2000 (MSA) requires that the Municipal Council, after\nconsultation with relevant stakeholders, must prepare and adopt the SDF as a core\ncomponent of their IDP.\n3.2 SDM is a Category C municipality in southern Gauteng, South Africa, covering 4,173 km² and\nknown as the "Vaal". The district municipality is located in the southern extent of the Gauteng\nProvince. The municipality comprises of the Lesedi, Midvaal and Emfuleni Local\nMunicipalities that form the jurisdiction of SDM. The district municipality is surrounded by\nthree provinces Free State to the south, Mpumalanga to the east and North West to the\nwestern part. It is the only part of the province situated on the banks of the Vaal River and\nVaal Dam.\n3.3 SDM is serviced by key national and provincial routes acting as major economic corridors,\nprimarily the N1 (Johannesburg-Bloemfontein), N3 (Johannesburg-Durban), N17\n(Johannesburg-Ermelo) and the R59 (Alberton-Sasolburg). Other crucial arterial routes\n\nfacilitating local and regional movement include the R42, R54, R82 and the R553 (Golden\nHighway).\n1. THE LEGISLATIVE FRAMEWORK\n4.1 The Spatial Planning and Land Use Management Act (SPLUMA)\nSPLUMA is a legislation that gives a framework for all spatial planning and land use\nmanagement in South Africa. It seeks to promote consistency and uniformity in procedures\nand decision-making pertaining to issues such as spatial planning and land use management.\nOther objectives include addressing historical spatial imbalances and the integration of the\nprinciples of sustainable development into land use, planning regulatory tools and legislative\ninstruments.\n4.1.1 SPLUMA requires national, provincial, and municipal spheres of government to prepare\nSDFs that establish a clear vision that must be developed through a thorough inventory and\nanalysis based on national spatial planning principles and local long-term development goals\nand plans. Section 12(2) of SPLUMA confirms that all three spheres must participate in each\nother’s processes of spatial planning and land use management and each sphere must be\nguided by its own SDF when taking decisions relating to land use and development.\n4.1.2 Chapter 4 Part A of SPLUMA sets out the focus and general requirements that must guide\nthe preparation and compilation of SDF products at various scales. Section 20 and 21 gives\na clear requirement for the amendment of municipal SDFs.\n4.1.3 An SDF adopted in terms of SPLUMA must guide and inform the exercise of any discretion\nor of any decision taken in terms of this Act or any other law relating to land use and\ndevelopment of land by that sphere of government.\n4.1.4 In the content of the SDF, Section 21 of SPLUMA states amongst others that a Municipal\nSDF must:\na) give effect to the development principles and applicable norms and standards set out in\nChapter 2.\nb) include a written and spatial representation of a five-year spatial development plan for the\nspatial form of the municipality.\n\nc) include a longer-term spatial development vision statement for the municipal area which\nindicates a desired spatial growth and development pattern for the next 10 to 20 years.\nd) identify current and future significant structuring and restructuring elements of the spatial\nform of the municipality, including development corridors, activity spines and economic\nnodes where public and private investment will be prioritized and facilitated.\ne) include population growth estimates for the next five years.\nf) include estimates of the demand for housing units across different socio-economic\ncategories and the planned location and density of future housing developments.\ng) include estimates of economic activity and employment trends and locations in the\nmunicipal area for the next five years.\nh) identify, quantify and provide location requirements of engineering infrastructure and\nservices provision for existing and future development needs for the next five years.\ni) identify the designated areas where national or provincial inclusionary housing policy may\nbe applicable.\nj) include a strategic assessment of the environmental pressures and opportunities within\nthe municipal area, including the spatial location of environmental sensitivities, high\npotential agricultural land and coastal access strips, where applicable.\nk) identify the designation of areas in the municipality where incremental upgrading\napproaches to development and regulation will be applicable.\nl) identify the designation of areas in which:\n(i) More detailed local plans must be developed\nm) provide the spatial expression of the coordination, alignment and integration of sectoral\npolicies of all municipal departments.\nn) determine a Capital Investment Framework (CIF) for the municipality’s development\nprogrammes, depicted spatially.\no) determine the purpose, desired impact and structure of the land use management scheme\nto apply in that municipal area; and\n\np) include an implementation plan, comprising of:\n(i) Sectoral requirements, including budgets and resources for implementation.\n(ii) Specification of institutional arrangements necessary for implementation.\n(iii) Specification of implementation targets, including dates and monitoring indicators;\nand\n(iv) Specification, where necessary, of any arrangements for partnerships in the\nimplementation process.\n4.2 The Municipal Systems Act, 32 of 2000 (MSA)\n4.2.1 The Local Government MSA, first introduced the concept of the Municipal SDF- as a\ncomponent of the mandatory IDP that every municipality must adopt.\n4.2.2 The provisions of the MSA that deal with Municipal SDFs must be read closely with the\nrelevant provisions, especially Part E, of SPLUMA. While the MSA establishes the core\nfeatures of the Municipal SDF, SPLUMA adds detailed provisions.\n4.2.3 Two elements of the MSA’s treatment of Municipal SDFs should be noted:\na) Chapter 5 of the Act deals with integrated development planning and provides the\nlegislative framework for the compilation and adoption of IDPs by municipalities.\nWithin the same chapter, section 26 (e), specifically requires an SDF as a mandatory\ncomponent of the municipal IDP. The rest of the chapter’s provisions on IDPs thus\napply to SDFs as well.\nb) In 2001, the Minister for Provincial and Local Government issued the Local\nGovernment: Municipal Planning and Performance Management Regulations. Within\nthese regulations, Regulation 2 (4), prescribes the minimum requirements for a\nMunicipal SDF.\n1.
