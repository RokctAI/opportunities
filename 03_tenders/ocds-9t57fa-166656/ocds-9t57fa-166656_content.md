1. PURPOSE OF THE PROJECT
The Johannes Phumani Phungula Local Municipality (JPPLM) seeks to appoint a suitably qualified
service provider to review the Strategic Environmental Assessment (SEA) adopted by Council in
1. The review must consider the current environmental realities, including the increasing effects
of climate change, by applying a climate change lens within the SEA methodology. This does not
constitute the development of a Climate Change Response Plan, but rather ensures that climate risks,
vulnerabilities and resilience considerations are integrated into the updated SEA.
The review should further introduce and assess the concept of ecological infrastructure as part of the
municipality’s environmental assets. Ecological infrastructure refers to naturally functioning
ecosystems that provide essential ecosystem goods and services and is increasingly recognised as
critical to sustainable spatial planning and climate resilience.
The updated SEA will support informed municipal decision-making, promote sustainable
development, guide land use planning, and strengthen the alignment between the SEA, Strategic
Environmental Management Plan (SEMP) and the municipal Spatial Development Framework
(SDF).
1. BACKGROUND
The JPPLM developed the SEA that was adopted by the Municipal Council in 2018. Six years have
passed since the adoption of the SEA hence the need for the review. The development of the existing
SEA culminated in the Strategic Environmental Management Plan (SEMP) product. Therefore, the
review of the SEA needs also produced a reviewed SEMP. The municipal Spatial Development
Framework (SDF) will be assessed and aligned through the SEA process. The alignment will form
basis for strategic spatial planning in the municipality by identifying areas where development is
compatible, restricted or unsuited to conditions contained in the guidelines and Environmental
Control Zones of the SEA. The SEA will assist the municipality in ensuring that sensitive areas such
as areas with critical biodiversity, ecological infrastructure, floodlines, and protected areas are set
aside, protected and restored, where applciable. It will also identify areas where the municipality
should thread carefully.
The Harry Gwala District’s Environmental Management Framework (EMF) should serve as an
environmental management guideline for local municipalities in Harry Gwala District, the Spatial
Planning and Land Use Management Act of 2013 and Municipal Systems Act of 2000 requires that
each local municipality undertake the strategic assessment of their Spatial Development Framework
proposals on the environment using the SEA process. The development process of an SEMP at a
municipal level should be aligned to the recommendations of an EMF at a district level.
SEA is promoted in policy and legislation in South Africa in a number of ways, which include the
following:
• Chapter 5 of South Africa’s National Environmental Management Act (NEMA) No 107 of
1998 makes provision for the development of procedures for the assessment of the impact of
policies, plans and programmes.
• The Municipal Systems Act No 32 of 2000 provides the context for municipal planning in
South Africa. This Act (Ch5 s25(1)) requires that local authorities adopt a single plan that
integrates development plans ; aligns the resources and capacity of the municipality and forms
the policy framework on which annual budgets are based. The Municipal Planning and
Performance Regulations (Ch2, s2(4)(f)) promulgated in 2001 in terms of this Act, state that
Page 10 of 80

a strategic assessment must be undertaken of the environmental impact of the spatial
development framework contained in the municipality’s plan.
• The White Paper on Spatial Planning and Land Use Management (2001) states that each
municipality in South Africa must compile a Spatial Development Framework (SDF). This
framework should guide and inform all decisions of the municipality related to the use,
development and planning of land (Ministry of Agriculture and Land Affairs, 2001). One of
the four components of this framework, listed in section 3.2 of the White Paper, is a strategic
environmental assessment.
• Spatial Planning and Land Use Management Act No 16 of 2013 states that a municipal SDF
must include a strategic assessment of the environmental pressures and opportunities within
the municipal area, including the spatial location of environmental sensitivities, high potential
agricultural land and coastal access strips, where applicable.
2.1 Study Area
JPPLM is situated within the Harry Gwala District Municipality which was previously known as the
Sisonke District Municipality. The main administrative centre of the municipality is the town of
Ixopo, which is located circa 85 km south-east of Pietermaritzburg. It borders onto the Dr Nkosazana
Dlamini-Zuma, Richmond, Vulamehlo, Umzumbe and Umzimkhulu local municipalities. It covers
an area of approximately 1604 km². The Ixopo town makes up the primary development node of the
municipality.
The Municipality has strong north-south linkages and east-west linkages within its region. This is
achieved via R56, which links it to areas such as Pietermaritzburg to the north and Kokstad to the
south. R612 provides regional access and linkages with the South Coast tourism region in the east
and Southern Drakensburg to the west. In addition, JPPLM is located at the intersection of at least
three established tourism regions, namely: Southern Drakensberg; Natal Midlands; and Ugu South
Coast.
JPP local authority consist of 14 wards with an urban area of Ixopo situated within Wards 2 and 4.
Ixopo is strategically located at the intersection of the R612 and R56 acts a regional hub which is
gradually regaining and establishing itself to be a noted sub-regional service centre for the
surrounding rural communities. There are various Traditional Authorities existing within the area as
well as commercial farms surrounding the Town of Ixopo.
1. OBJECTIVE OF THE PROJECT
The overall objective is to conduct a comprehensive review of the 2018 SEA to ensure that it remains
relevant, scientifically sound, and aligned with current environmental conditions, legislation and
spatial planning policies. The review must also ensure that climate change related considerations and
ecological infrastructure are integrated within the SEA framework while maintaining the original
purpose and scope of an SEA.
The specific objectives of the SEA review are to:
• Update and strengthen the environmental base through defining and spatially representing
environmentally sensitive areas, including critical biodiversity areas, ecological support
areas, wetlands, floodlines, rivers, riparian buffers, protected areas and priority ecological
infrastructure (the current state of the environment in the study area).
o Integrate ecological infrastructure into the identification of environmental
opportunities and constraints.
Page 11 of 80

• Identify environmental opportunities and constraints including the areas in need of protection
and restoration and areas having activities that are potential threats to the environment.
o Integrate climate vulnerability and resilience considerations into environmental
opportunities and constraints.
• Through consultation with relevant interested and affected parties, determine the desired state
of the environment for the study area including establishing environmental sustainability
objectives, criteria, targets and indicators.
• Update the Strategic Environmental Management Plan to address current environmental
priorities, including ecological infrastructure protection and restoration and climate-aware
environmental management actions.
o Enhance the management and conservation of environmentally sensitive areas,
ecological infrastructure and high potential agricultural assets within the
municipality.
o Provide clear environmental management zones (EMZs)
o Provide clear guidance on suitable and unsuitable areas for development based on
environmental sensitivity and climate-responsive considerations.
o To provide practical strategies on how the municipality can sustain the continued
benefits of ecosystem goods and services provided by vulnerable ecological resources
in the local municipality.
o Recommend practical interventions that support sustainable development and
improved environmental decision-making.
• Strengthen alignment with municipal and district planning instruments
o Ensure alignment with the updated Spatial Development Framework (SDF), Land Use
Scheme (LUS), and Integrated Development Plan (IDP).
o Align the SEA and SEMP with the Harry Gwala District EMF and other relevant
national and provincial environmental legislation and tools.
• Support municipal decision-making
o Provide environmental guidance that assists with development control, land use
applications, environmental authorisation processes, strategic planning and project
screening.
o Improve the municipality’s ability to anticipate environmental risks, especially those
worsened by climate change.
o Enhance coordinated environmental management across departments.
• Develop environmental monitoring and reporting systems to measure the success and
implementation of the above-mentioned tools.
1. SCOPE AND EXTENT OF WORK
The appointed service provider is expected to undertake a comprehensive review of the 2018 SEA
and develop an updated SEA and SEMP that reflect current environmental conditions, spatial
planning needs, emerging climate-related risks, and the importance of ecological infrastructure. The
scope includes the following tasks:

The Inception Report will result from the commissioning/inception meeting. The purpose of the
inception meeting is for the service provider to reach agreement with the municipality on the process,
approach, methodology, time frames, deliverables, work plan, reporting schedule and general
requirements and expectations of the project. Roles and responsibilities should also be outlined in the
Inception Report. The identification of the stakeholders to form part of the Project Steering
Committee will also be undertaken during the inception meeting.

4.2 PHASE 2: SITUATIONAL ANALYSIS REPORT
The Situational Analysis Report will provide the current state of the environment of JPPLM. It should
be informed by, amongst other factors, the:
• The review and analyze the following (where applicable):
o The 2018 SEA and SEMP.
o JPPLM Spatial Development Framework (SDF).
o JPPLM Land Use Scheme (LUS).
o JPPLM Integrated Development Plan (IDP).
o Harry Gwala District Environmental Management Framework (EMF).
o Any available municipal climate change information (to be used only to guide
environmental sensitivity and vulnerability, not to create a Climate Change Response
Plan).
o Relevant national and provincial environmental legislation, policies and guidelines.
o All available GIS datasets related to biodiversity, hydrology, ecological infrastructure,
flood-risk zones, climate exposure indices, etc.
• Defining and spatially depicting the study area
• Stakeholder engagement -.
• Conduct relevant specialist studies
• Undertake ground truthing – the study should not merely be a desktop study. Ground truthing
will assist in ensuring that that the Status Quo findings are accurate and reflects what is really
happening on the ground.
4.3 PHASE 3: DESIRED END STATE
This phase should begin by determining key findings from the status quo, their relevance to the SEA
and the municipality. Furthermore, it should enlist the drivers and pressures that are causing the issue
to be prevalent and the result that have manifested as a result. Environmental constraints and
opportunities to development should also be outlined.
The Desired State of the Environment (DSoE) for JPPLM should be developed in the format of a
Sustainability Framework which the Municipality can then use to assess the sustainability of all
Policies, Plans and Programmes in the future. The DSoE should be informed by existing limits of
acceptable change, the vision, a series of sustainability objectives and criteria which are designed to
address issues raised in the Status Quo.
4.4 PHASE 4: ENVIRONMENTAL CONTROL ZONES AND GUIDELINES
The Environmental Control Zones (ECZs) are intended as a tool to guide and inform future spatial
planning while enabling for the screening of the proposed developments. ECZs will provide a spatial
and qualified indication of areas within which development options are recommended, restricted or
undesirable based on the consideration of the conservation and land use categories. Working in
conjunction with the Sustainability Framework, these provide an added dimension to the SEA that
will help the municipality to move towards achieving the sustainability vision.
4.5 PHASE 5: DRAFT STRATEGIC ENVIRONMENTAL MANAGEMENT PLAN
This phase will identify development trends and strategic land use patterns and conflicts. This will
be informed by the review of planning tools, SDF and Land Use Management Scheme (LUMS), as
well as the Capital Investment Framework (CIF). The intention is for the outcomes from the SEA to
be a key input into the SDF, LUMS and CIF and form the basis for assessing the sustainability of the
development path proposed in these plans in relation to ecological thresholds.
Assessment of development scenarios should be undertaken. The development trends should be
compared to the constraints and opportunities discussed in Phase 3. In so doing, potential impacts of
Page 13 of 80

development trends will be identified. Recommendations and alternatives should be suggested to
mitigate impacts.
4.6 PHASE 6: FINAL STRATEGIC ENVIRONMENTAL MANAGEMENT PLAN
(SEMP)
The SEMP developed will give effect to the implementation of the environmental vision, specific
objectives and goals identified in the Desired End State. It will determine strategies to achieve the
desired state of the environment and sustainable development in accordance with the determined
sustainability objectives. Furthermore, the SEMP will establish the action/implementation plan to
achieve strategies, as well as determining the responsibilities, timeframe, milestones and anticipated
resources required. This phase should also make reference to all the relevant legal, administrative and
policy frameworks within which the SEMP and the municipal functions must operate.
4.7 PHASE 7: MONITORING AND EVALUATION PLAN
The focus should be on the development of the system to monitor, evaluate and report on the progress
made towards improving the state of environment in the municipality and to measure the success of
the implementation of the strategies.
