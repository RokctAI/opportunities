2. BACKGROUND INFORMATION
2.1. Introduction
The Container Corridor (ConCor) is the backbone of South Africa’s overall rail freight network.
it connects and interfaces with the North Corridor, Cape Corridor, and Central Corridor. This
Corridor is a critical logistics enabler for South Africa's import and export freight transportation,
as well as a key contributor to the country's economic growth. It represents a significant
opportunity for rail-based General Freight Business growth, especially as the private sector
develops more Logistics Hubs and Back of Port facilities along the Corridor.
ConCor connects the Port of Durban to Gauteng's economic hub and offers direct rail transport
of strategic commodities from the Port of Durban to the inland terminals (City Deep, Kascon,
Pretcon, Kaalfontein), and several private sidings throughout Gauteng and beyond.
Furthermore, this corridor serves as a critical logistical enabler of South Africa's import and
export freight transportation, as well as an important component in the country's and region's
economic growth strategy.
The Container Corridor Durban cluster runs up to Stanger, connecting with the North Corridor
and down the South Coast to Port Shepstone, and from Pietermaritzburg to Newcastle and
then Johannesburg in the Gauteng Province. Commodities railed on the Container Corridor
include containers, automotive, grain, fuel, chemicals, coal, manganese, chrome, and FMCG
general freight cargo and, Pietermaritzburg area largely consists of branch lines where most
of the activity is from the Timber sector. The dominant industries serviced are the Container,
Automotive, Agriculture, and Fuel sectors. The Gauteng cluster handles container cargo and
operates the major inland container terminals of City Deep and Kascon. Containers move
between the Port of Durban, the Cape Corridor, Botswana, and Pretoria.
Container Corridor key facts:
• Lifeblood of the Gauteng and Durban economy, consequently the country at large;
• Domestic, import and export traffic transported;
• Eskom coal trains and Richard’s Bay – Glencoe coal trains;
• Contributes to the GDP of the country; and
• Reduces reliance on road transport.
However, the terminal has faced challenges in recent years. Freight volumes transported on
the Corridor decreased from 12.6 Mtpa in 2017/18 to 6.7 Mtpa in 2024/25, resulting in a
revenue drop from R2.6 bn to R1.3 bn as shown in Figure 1 below.

Figure 1: Historical Performance
Several factors have compounded to reduce the operational efficiency of rail deliveries to the
port:
• Locomotive and Spare Parts Shortage: There has been a chronic shortage of
locomotives and necessary spare parts, which has directly impacted the frequency and
reliability of commodity deliveries by rail to the Port of Durban.
• Cable Theft and Vandalism: The rail infrastructure has suffered from frequent
incidents of cable theft and vandalism. These disruptions have caused considerable
delays and uncertainties in rail transport, leading to inefficiencies in scheduling and
logistics.
• Infrastructure Degradation: General degradation of the rail infrastructure without
timely maintenance or upgrades has further exacerbated the problem, leading to an
increase in the number of derailments and reduced rail capacity and reliability.
The decline in rail performance and subsequent impact on commodity export volumes
underline the critical need for robust rail infrastructure to support major export facilities like the
Port of Durban.
The limited capital available in Transnet has significantly impacted its ability to maintain and
expand its rail infrastructure, which in turn has affected industries reliant on this network, such
as commodity exports & imports through the Port of Durban. The situation calls for a strategic
review and urgent maintenance investment in the commodity export rail line.
To understand where to invest in prioritised track maintenance, Container Corridor is
considering deploying the above-ground and below-ground asset condition scan to audit the
current state of repair of the Container Corridor. This track forms a vital corridor for the
movement of commodity from Johannesburg to Durban.
A non-destructive survey is believed that it could be a significant step towards addressing the
ongoing issues of track degradation. It will provide stakeholders with a baseline of the current
state of repair of the track, including above-ground assets like sleepers, rail, ditches and
earthworks as well as below-ground track bed assets such as ballast and sub-ballast layers
and the formation. This evidence will allow stakeholders to make informed decisions when

planning essential maintenance work. The register and accompanying work order
recommendations can help prioritise future work to upgrade and maintain the network to
achieve the nameplate capacity. The information would also be invaluable to help engineers
devise an implementation plan with priority sections to improve maintenance performance and
asset reliability.
The current scope of works includes all tracks (1404km double line) between Johannesburg
(City Deep) and Durban.
The survey initiative would be a significant step towards addressing the ongoing issues of track
degradation. Here’s how this could help:
• Diagnosis of Rail Issues: By identifying areas of wear, corrosion, or other structural
weaknesses, the survey can pinpoint specific problems that need attention.
• Preventative Maintenance: The data from a survey can inform preventative
maintenance strategies. By addressing issues early, Transnet Rail Infrastructure
Manager can prevent minor problems from escalating into major failures that disrupt
commodity transport from Johannesburg to Port of Durban.
• Efficiency and Safety Improvements: Regularly scheduled surveys can help
ensure that the rail infrastructure is maintained to a high standard, improving the
overall efficiency and safety of the rail network. This is crucial for maintaining a
reliable supply chain for commodity exports & imports.
• Cost Savings: While the initial investment in such technology and surveys might
seem high, the long-term cost savings from avoiding large-scale rail failures and
unscheduled downtime can be substantial.
• Supporting Expansion Plans: With the Durban Port looking to expand capacity
and possibly integrate more sophisticated logistics solutions, having a robust rail
infrastructure is essential. The survey initiative in maintaining and upgrading the
railway tracks is vital for these future plans.
This outcome could serve as a model for how technology and expert analysis can be leveraged
to improve critical infrastructure, ensuring the longevity and reliability of essential transport
networks.

3. SCOPE OF WORK/TERMS OF REFERENCE
3.1. Scope overview
This project involves conducting a comprehensive track survey along the section between
Gauteng and Durban, covering a double-line route with a total extent of 1,404 track kilometres.
The survey is intended to establish a clear baseline of the current condition of the railway
infrastructure. It will assess both above-ground assets—such as rails, sleepers, drainage
ditches, and earthworks—and below-ground components, including the ballast, sub-ballast
layers, and track formation. The findings will provide robust, evidence-based information to
enable stakeholders to make informed decisions regarding the planning and prioritisation of
essential maintenance and rehabilitation activities. This will entail the following deliverables:
i. Mobilisation of the survey equipment to Johannesburg (City Deep)
ii. Survey of the network from Gauteng to Port of Durban and back to Gauteng
iii. Demobilisation of the survey equipment
iv. Data capturing and processing of the data collected
v. Analyze survey data to produce tailored, actionable work‑order recommendations.
vi. Provide desktop-based viewing software to enable the synchronisation of survey data,
analysis of results, and reporting. The deliverable includes full system access, user
training, and ongoing technical support for five (5) designated Transnet officials for the full
duration of the project.
3.2. Survey Equipment Capabilities
The surveying system shall be a rugged, integrated unit incorporating all required cameras
and sensor technologies within a single platform. The system shall be fully self‑contained,
including its own power supply, environmental control, onboard processing, and data
storage with backup capability. It shall be designed to reliably collect time‑synchronised
track bed inspection data under operational railway conditions. The system shall be
deployable in a containerised configuration suitable for installation on a rail wagon or the
survey equipment can be fitted onto a dedicated road rail vehicle. The configuration will
undergo internal Transnet approval and Railway Safety Regulatory approvals to ensure
that it can safely operate on the section between Gauteng and Durban.
3.3. Detailed scope of work
3.3.1. Mobilisation of the Survey Equipment to Johannesburg (City Deep)
Mobilisation will include the secure transportation of calibrated survey equipment, sensors, data
acquisition units, and supporting hardware to the City Deep mobilisation point in Johannesburg.
All equipment will be inspected, tested, and verified against manufacturer specifications and
applicable industry standards prior to deployment. A mobilisation checklist, asset register, and
calibration certificates will be submitted to confirm readiness and compliance. Mobilisation
activities will be executed in accordance with regulated safety, security, and access‑control
requirements.

3.3.2. Survey of the Network from Gauteng to the Port of Durban and Return to Gauteng
A comprehensive condition and geometry survey will be conducted along the designated rail
corridor between Gauteng and the Port of Durban, including the return leg. The survey will
collect continuous, geo‑referenced data on track geometry, alignment, profile, and observable
infrastructure defects using instrumented survey systems. Data collection will be executed at
operational speeds where permitted, minimising disruption to rail operations. Survey activities
will comply with applicable Transnet engineering standards, rail safety regulations, and
operational protocols.
3.3.3. Demobilisation of the Survey Equipment
Upon completion of the fieldwork, all survey equipment will be systematically demobilised from
site. This includes safe shutdown, data integrity verification, packaging, and transportation of
equipment back to the designated storage or maintenance facility. A demobilisation report will
confirm asset condition, data completeness, and any maintenance requirements identified
post-survey.
3.3.4. Data Capturing and Processing of the Data Collected
To ensure consistency between data collection, processing, and reporting outputs, the captured
and validated survey data will be structured to support a suite of standardised condition and
asset assessment reports. Following data cleansing, quality assurance, route chainage

reports outlined in Table 1. These reports represent the minimum expected deliverables derived
from the integrated use of GPR, optical imaging, inertial, and positioning technologies, and
collectively support infrastructure condition assessment, asset management, and
decision‑making. Additional reports may be produced where required.

3.3.5. Analysis of Survey Data and Development of Actionable Work‑Order
Recommendations
Processed survey data will be analysed by experienced engineering and asset‑management
specialists to identify defects, emerging trends, and priority intervention areas. The analysis will
convert raw measurements into a structured defect register and practical, risk‑based work‑order
recommendations aligned with maintenance thresholds, safety requirements, performance
targets, and nameplate capacity objectives. These outputs will support the prioritisation of future
upgrade and maintenance works and provide engineers with the evidence required to develop
a phased implementation plan focusing on critical network sections, improved maintenance
performance, and enhanced asset reliability.
3.3.6. Provision of Desktop‑Based Viewing Software, Training, and Support
A desktop‑based viewing and analysis software solution will be provided to enable the
synchronisation, interrogation, visualisation, and reporting of survey data and analytical results.
The deliverable includes full system access for five (5) designated Transnet officials,
comprehensive user training (covering data navigation, interpretation, and reporting), and
ongoing technical support for the full duration of the project. The software will support secure
data access, role‑based permissions, and export of reports suitable for engineering,
maintenance, and management use.

the right to disqualify a bidder from further participation in the tender process. Disqualification on this
