SECTION A: INTRODUCTION AND SCOPE OF WORK
1. Introduction
About ATNS
The Air Traffic and Navigation Services (ATNS) Company of South Africa provides air traffic
management, communication, surveillance, navigation, and related services, including
training. ATNS manages 6% of the world’s airspace and employs over 1,100 staff to ensure
safe, efficient, and orderly air traffic services across 21 aerodromes in South Africa,
including OR Tambo, Cape Town, and King Shaka International Airports. In the broader African
region, ATNS supports aeronautical satellite communication (VSAT networks) across 33
states, connecting the continent from Cape to Cairo and extending to the Middle East.
ATNS Vision:
To be the leading provider of air traffic management solutions and associated services across
Africa and select international markets.
ATNS Mission:
To provide safe, expeditious, and efficient air traffic management solutions, while ensuring
economic, social, and environmental sustainability.
ATNS values include:
Safety and Customer Centricity: Prioritising customer needs and ensuring that
safety is non-negotiable.
Accountability: Holding ourselves and others accountable for our actions
Agility: Ensuring that we are flexible and adaptable to change
Diversity: Embracing inclusion, equality and social differences
Integrity: Following a moral and incorruptible corporate code

ATNS Business Environment
ATNS is a State-Owned Company (SOC), established in 1993 under the ATNS Company Act
(Act 45 of 1993) to provide air traffic services aligned with ICAO standards and South African
Civil Aviation Regulations. As a commercialised air navigation service provider (ANSP), ATNS
operates on a “user-pays” principle, relying on revenues and debt funding to cover operational
and capital expenses.
Regulated Business Activities
ATNS regulated activities contribute 90% of its revenue. Key offerings include:
Air navigation services: Planning, operating, and maintaining airspace infrastructure
such as communication, navigation, and surveillance (CNS) systems.
Air traffic service charges: Governed by the Economic Regulating Committee (RC),
ATNS sets service tariffs and maintains service standards under a five-year permission
structure.
Training: The ATNS Aviation Training Academy (ATA) provides internationally
accredited air traffic services and technical training, earning recognition as IATA’s Top
Regional Training Partner in 2012 and 2013.
Non-Regulated Business Activities:
ATNS non-regulated operations contribute 10% of revenue and focus on regional
expansion through a subsidiary, ATNS International. This platform enables ATNS to
explore joint ventures and partnerships, enhancing market opportunities and
regional influence.
For more details, visit: www.atns.com

1. Purpose of the Bid
2.1. The primary objective of this project is to appoint a qualified and experienced service
provider to deliver comprehensive commercial electrical maintenance services at the
Aviation Training Academy for a period of twelve (12) months, on an as and when
required basis.
2.2. The appointed service provider will ensure that all electrical infrastructure, systems,
and equipment are maintained in a safe, reliable, efficient, and compliant condition to
support uninterrupted operations, minimize downtime, ensure regulatory compliance,
and extend the lifespan of electrical assets while adhering to applicable industry
standards and safety regulations.
2.3. Scope of Work
2.3.1 Electrical system assessment
(a) The service provider will conduct assessment of the building electrical systems
as and when required.
(b) The service provider will identify and document any existing faults with the
lighting and other building electrical circuits.
2.3.2 Faut finding
(a) The service provider will conduct fault finding and identify the root cause of any
lighting and overall building circuits faults.
(b) The service provider will be required to develop an action plan to rectify the
identified faults.
2.3.3 Electrical circuits serving
(a) The service provider is to ensure the building electrical systems are functioning
properly.
(b) The service provider is to ensure that all the faults identified are resolved.
2.3.4 Replacement of contractors and breakers.

(a) The service provider will be required to replace contactors and breakers as and when
necessary.
(b) The service provider is to ensure that all replacements are installed in compliance
with industry standards and manufacturer guidelines.
2.3.5 Certificate of compliance
(a) The service provider will be required to issue a certificate of compliance after
servicing or maintenance has been conducted.
(b) The service provider will ensure that the certificate of compliance will include a
detailed description/list of all work carried out and is compliant with all relevant
regulations and standards.
2.3.5 Lighting
(a) The service provider removing and replacing existing Light Emitting Diode (LED)
panel light and downlight and any other fixtures will be required to dispose them
appropriately. A destruction certificate shall be used after every work requiring
disposal.
(b) The service provider is to ensure all light replaced are secure.
(c) The service provider will be required to repair/replace or install external
lights/floodlights as and when required
2.3.7 Reporting
(a) The service provider will be required to provide a detailed report of all maintenance
tasks performed to the appointed ATNS representative. The report is to be provided
after completion of the service. The report is also to detail any future
recommendations or repairs.
2.3.8 Warranty
(a) The service provider will be required to provide a detailed report of all maintenance
tasks performed to the appointed ATNS representative. The report is to be provided
after completion of the service. The report is also to detail any future
recommendations or repairs
2.3.9 Schedule

(a) The service provider will be required to work according to the following
schedules with different rates applicable to each.
• Monday to Friday 08:00-16:00
• Monday to Friday 16:00-08:00
• Saturdays
• Public holidays and Sundays
2.3.10 Response times
(a) The service provider will be required to respond to emergencies within 1 to 2
hours.
2.3.11 Projectors
(a) Repair and or replacement of projectors as and when requires on a quotation
basis
2.3.12 UPS
(a) Service provider will be required to repair, replace batteries/UPS equipment in
cases of complete failure.
2.3.13 Additional work
(a) Service provider will be required to install appliances and electrical equipment
not mentioned on the schedule as an when required on an AD-Hoc basis
2.3.14 Kitchen
(a) The service provider will also be required to service and or repair kitchen
stoves and all the equipment in the area that operates on electrical power
within the building except for fire installations.
(b) Servicing power supplies to stoves, fridges, food warmers, hydroboils,
geysers etc.
2.3.15. Building renovations

(a) Service provider to work installing power to new building renovations.
(b) Installing electrical equipment.
2.3.15 Energy saving
(a) The service provider will be required to assist and use energy efficient
equipment, lighting and other systems not mentioned in the scope.
2.3.16 Health and safety
(a) The service provider shall comply with all health and safety requirements and
regulations set forth by national and local municipalities.
(b) The service provider is to ensure that all the tools and equipment used on site are in

32.2. A local supplier shall be entirely responsible for all taxes, duties, license fees, etc.,

32.3. No contract shall be concluded with any bidder whose tax matters are not in order.
Prior to the award of a bid the Department must be in possession of a tax clearance
certificate, submitted by the bidder. This certificate must be an original issued by the
South African Revenue Services.
1. National Industrial Participation (NIP) Programme
33.1. The NIP Programme administered by the Department of Trade and Industry shall be
applicable to all contracts that are subject to the NIP obligation.
1. Prohibition of Restrictive practices
34.1. In terms of section 4 (1) (b) (iii) of the Competition Act No. 89 of 1998, as amended,
an agreement between, or concerted practice by, firms, or a decision by an association
of firms, is prohibited if it is between parties in a horizontal relationship and if a bidder
(s) is / are or a contractor(s) was / were involved in collusive bidding (or bid rigging).
34.2. If a bidder(s) or contractor(s), based on reasonable grounds or evidence obtained by
the purchaser, has / have engaged in the restrictive practice referred to above, the
purchaser may refer the matter to the Competition Commission for investigation and
possible imposition of administrative penalties as contemplated in the Competition Act
No. 89 of 1998.
34.3. If a bidder(s) or contractor(s), has / have been found guilty by the Competition
Commission of the restrictive practice referred to above, the purchaser may, in addition
and without prejudice to any other remedy provided for, invalidate the bid(s) for such
item(s) offered, and / or terminate the contract in whole or part, and / or restrict the
bidder(s) or contractor(s) from conducting business with the public sector for a period
not exceeding ten (10) years and / or claim damages from the bidder(s) or contractor(s)
concerned.
