Requiring bidders to permanently employ key professionals
and provide three months' proof of employment-related
statutory contributions before the outcome of the tender is
known exposes SMMEs to substantial financial risk. Many
emerging firms cannot reasonably sustain the cost of
permanently employing highly skilled professionals without
the certainty of secured work. Consequently, this
requirement may inadvertently exclude capable and
competent firms from participating in the procurement
process. As a result, firms that have access to suitably
qualified professionals through letters of consent are
disadvantaged, notwithstanding their ability to mobilise these
resources immediately upon contract award.
The proposal does not adequately address the development
and empowerment of Exempted Micro Enterprises (EMEs)
and Qualifying Small Enterprises (QSEs), which are key
strategic objectives of SANRAL. It should explicitly
demonstrate how the project will contribute to the growth,
capacity building, and meaningful participation of EMEs and
QSEs within the value chain. Furthermore, the proposal
should outline practical measures to level the playing field for
emerging professionals by promoting equitable access to
opportunities, skills transfer, mentorship, and sustainable
professional development in line with SANRAL's
transformation and supplier development objectives.

We respectfully submit that this requirement may be
inconsistent with the fundamental principles governing public
procurement in South Africa, namely fairness, equity,
transparency, competitiveness, and cost-effectiveness.
Procurement requirements should be proportionate to the
objective they seek to achieve and should not unnecessarily
restrict competition or create barriers to participation,
particularly for SMMEs, whose development and inclusion are
recognised as important objectives within the public
procurement framework.
We therefore respectfully request that the permanent
employment requirement be reconsidered. As an alternative,
bidders could be permitted to submit signed letters of
consent from the proposed key personnel during the tender
stage, with the requirement to appoint such personnel on a
permanent basis becoming a condition of contract award or
contract commencement. This approach would adequately
protect the Employer's interests while ensuring a fair,
competitive, and inclusive procurement process.
We believe this proposal strikes an appropriate balance
between ensuring project delivery by committed key
personnel and promoting meaningful participation by
emerging engineering firms without exposing them to
unnecessary financial risk before contract award.
