SECTION 3: BACKGROUND, OVERVIEW AND SCOPE OF REQUIREMENTS
1 BACKGROUND
Transnet National Port Authority is responsible for the safe, effective, and efficient functioning of South Africa’s
eight (8) commercial ports. TNPA is also required to operate within a legislative and regulatory environment in
accordance with SOLAS, Maritime Safety Regulations, and other relevant regulations. The Marine Services
Department is responsible for discharging the following but not limited to:
• Safe, reliable, and efficient sea operations in the port.
• Planning and provision of maintenance and improvement of the functioning of the tugboats, pilot boats,
workboats, and launches.
• Ensure the provision of port services including the management of port activities.
• Ensure that there is proper maintenance for the manoeuvring of vessels within port limits.
For Transnet National Ports Authority (TNPA) to effectively plan replacement and refurbishment of its existing
marine craft fleet, a comprehensive condition assessment is essential. This assessment should systematically
evaluate each vessel’s operational readiness, structural integrity, mechanical performance, and compliance with
safety and environmental standards.
By establishing a detailed baseline of the current condition of tugs, pilot boats, and other service vessels, TNPA
can prioritize investment decisions, schedule maintenance more efficiently, and avoid unplanned service
disruptions. Furthermore, such assessments support lifecycle cost analysis and align with asset management best
practices, enabling the organization to optimize fleet performance while ensuring long-term financial sustainability
and service reliability across all South African ports under its jurisdiction
2 SCOPE OF REQUIREMENTS.
2.1 Transnet National Ports Authority seeks to appoint a service provider(s) to conduct a detailed condition assessment
for the four (04) Ports, to provide a full report accompanied by a detailed method statement and the cost estimate
of all the components deemed necessary to bring or maintain the crafts back into operations.
2.2 The scope of the assessment shall include the following but not limited to:
2.2.1 VIBRATION
Install and calibrate portable vibration monitoring equipment on critical rotating machinery.
• Conduct vibration readings at defined points on the following components:
• Main propulsion engines
• Auxiliary generators
• Gearboxes

2.1.1.1 Vibration
Install and calibrate portable vibration monitoring equipment on critical rotating machinery.
• Conduct vibration readings at defined points on the following components:
• Main propulsion engines
• Auxiliary generators
• Gearboxes
• Shaft bearings
• Pumps
• Analyse vibration data to detect abnormalities such as shaft misalignment, imbalance,
gear tooth wear, bearing defects, looseness in mounting or coupling systems.
• Provide a report with graphical plots, frequency spectra, and interpretation of all
vibration anomalies, along with recommended corrective actions.
2.1.1.2 DRIVE SHAFT ASSESSMENT
• Visually and physically inspect the drive shaft.
• Assess drive shaft for corrosion, surface wear, cracks, or scoring.
• Conduct precise shaft alignment checks between the engine, gearbox, and propeller.
• Misalignment tolerances should be measured and documented.
2

• Inspect shaft bearings for condition, lubrication status, and axial/radial play. Evaluate
coupling condition and torque transmission capacity.
2.1.1.3 MACHINERY CONDITION ASSESSMENT
• Conduct operational tests under varying loads to assess performance
parameters such as temperature, oil pressure, RPM stability, and exhaust
characteristics. Where possible obtain the latest report by OEM.
• Obtain latest oil analysis report not older than 1 month or request an order for
an analysis of oil samples for wear particles, water content, and chemical
degradation. Inspect lubrication system integrity (filters, pumps, lines). Review
reports and draw a summative conclusion of the lubrication system condition.
• Cooling System - Inspect heat exchangers, sea water strainers, coolant quality,

transfer.
• Fuel Systems Check - Inspect for leakage, blockage, or contamination in fuel
delivery systems. Include filters, injectors, and tanks.
• Electrical Systems - Assess power distribution, switchboards, emergency power.
