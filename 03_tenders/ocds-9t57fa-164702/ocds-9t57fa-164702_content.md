INTRODUCTION\n1.1 The South African Social Security Agency (“SASSA”) is a statutory body, established in\nterms of the South African Social Security Agency Act of 2004 as a schedule 3A\npublic entity in terms of the Public Finance Management Act of 1999 and mandated\nto ensure effective and efficient delivery of high-quality services with regards to\nmanagement and administration of social grants. Further, the Agency is mandated\nto ensure the entire processes and systems from application to receipt of social\ngrants is performed in a manner that is sensitive, caring and restores the dignity of\nbeneficiaries as well as the integrity of the social assistance system.\n1.2 SASSA operates through 9 regions with each regional office in charge of the\nadministration and management of social assistance within its locality.\n1.3 Amongst the products offered by the Agency are disability related grants which require\napplicants to be subjected to assessments conducted by Medical/ Health\npractitioners.\n1.4 These disability related grants include:\na. The Disability Grant, which in terms of section 9(b) of the Social Assistance Act\nof 2004, as amended, is awarded to “any person, who is, owing to a\nphysical or mental disability, unfit to obtain by virtue of any service,\nemployment or profession the means needed to enable him or her to\nprovide for his or her maintenance”. This grant can be awarded on a\ntemporary basis for a period of between 6 and 12 months after which the grant\nis lapsed or a permanent basis to those in which the disability is expected to last\nfor longer than 12 consecutive months.\nb. The Care Dependency Grant (CDG), which in terms of the section 7(a)of the\nSocial Assistance Act of 2004, as amended, is awarded to “…a parent,\nprimary care giver or foster parent of a child who requires and receives\npermanent care or support services due to his or her physical or mental\ndisability”.\nc. The DG and CDG may be reviewed at any time where there are reasons to\nbelieve that the medical circumstances of the beneficiary might have changed\nor the beneficiary previously awarded in error or fraudulent means.\nd. The Grant in Aid, which in terms of section 9(b) of the Social Assistance Act of\n2004, as amended, is awarded to a person who “…is in such a physical or\nmental condition that he or she requires regular attendance by another\nperson”.\n\n10\n1. THE OBJECTIVES OF THE DISABILITY POST-APPLICATION QUALITY\nASSURANCE PROJECT\n2.1 To identify clients who may have been incorrectly recommended for a grant. These\nclients will undergo a grants review process, which includes a new disability\nassessment conducted by a different health practitioner that the one who performed\nthe initial assessment.\n2.2 Results of the post-application TQA to be utilized by regional teams to inform\nretraining initiatives, profile assessors and facilitate individual one-on-one sessions\nbetween assessors and the Regional Disability Management representatives.\n2.3 To identify disability assessors who do not adhere to set performance standards\nwith respect to disability assessments.\n2.4 Where possible determine potential fraud indicators for disability related grants.\n1.
