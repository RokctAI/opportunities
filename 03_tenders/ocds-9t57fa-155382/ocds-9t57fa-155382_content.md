VERIFICATION OF B-BBEE LEVELS IN RESPECT OF EMEs
In terms of the Generic Codes of Good Practice, an enterprise including a sole propriety
with annual total revenue of R10 million or less qualifies as an EME.
In instances where Sector Charters are developed to address the transformation challenges
of specific sectors or industries, the threshold for qualification as an EME may be different
from the generic threshold of R10 million. The relevant Sector Charter thresholds will
therefore be used as a basis for a potential bidder to qualify as an EME.
• For example, the approved thresholds for EMEs for the Tourism and Construction
Sector Charters are R2.5 million and R1.5 million respectively.
• An EME automatically qualifies as a level 4 contributor with B-BBEE recognition level
of 100% in terms of the Codes of Good Practice.
• An EME with at least 51% black ownership qualifies as Level 2 Contributor with B-
BBEE level of 125% in terms of the Codes of Good Practice.
• An EME with 100% black ownership qualifies as a Level 1 contributor with B-BBEE
level of 135% in terms of the Codes of Good Practice.
• An EME that is regarded as a specialized enterprise with at least 75% black
beneficiaries qualifies as Level 1 contributor with B-BBEE level of 135% in terms of
Codes of Good Practice.
• An EME that is regarded as a specialized enterprise with at least 51% black
beneficiaries qualifies as a Level 2 contributor with B-BBEE level of 125% in terms of
the Codes of Good Practice.
• An EME is required to submit a sworn affidavit confirming their annual total revenue of
R 10 million or less and level of black ownership to claim points
• An EME that is regarded as a Specialized Enterprise, is required to submit a sworn
affidavit confirming their annual turnover/ allocated budget/ gross receipt of R 10 million
or less and level of percentage of black beneficiaries to claim points
• An EME may be measured in terms of the QSE scorecard should they wish to
maximize their points and move to a higher B-BBEE recognition level. It is in this
context that an EME may submit a B-BBEE verification certificate.

1. INTRODUCTION AND BACKGROUND
1.1 The Cape Winelands District Municipality (CWDM) seeks to appoint a suitably qualified
service provider to undertake a partial revision of its Staff Establishment. The municipality
completed a comprehensive organisational review and full staff establishment revision during
2023/2024 in accordance with the Municipal Staff Regulations (MSR) No. 890 and MSR
Guidelines No. 891 issued under the Municipal Systems Act, 2000. This revised staff
establishment was implemented on 1 July 2024. A detailed diagnostic report stemming from
that process currently exists and remains fully aligned with the MSR requirements.
1.2 In April 2025, CWDM was formally notified that the Roads Agency function will transfer to the
Western Cape Department of Infrastructure effective 1 April 2026, which has now been
extended for another 3 months until 30 June 2026. This major functional change
necessitates a targeted partial revision of the staff establishment to ensure continued
organisational capability, functional compliance and structural alignment post transfer.
1.3 Additionally, the suitably qualified service provider must assess structural requirements in the
Division: Internal Audit in order to strengthen accountability and align with statutory
requirements; assess the need for additional capacity, including administrative support for
the Executive Director: Corporate Services and a focused organisational and capacity review
in the Division: Facilities Management; all the afore stated must be incorporated into the
partial review.
1.4 The service provider must assess the organisational impact of transferring the Division:
Roads (294 positions out of the total 595 municipal employees) in terms of:
• staff establishment configuration
• functional and operational capacity
• service delivery impact
• organisational performance implications.
• necessary structural improvements to the Division: Internal Audit
• Assessment of the adequacy of administrative support functions, including workload
analysis and role alignment for the Executive Director: Corporate Services
• Targeted assessment of the Division: Facilities Management to determine:
o the appropriateness of the current organisational structure
o whether restructuring is required to improve service delivery and operational
efficiency
o the need for additional capacity, including administrative and technical support.
1.5 The assignment must assess—against constitutional and legislative mandates for District
Municipalities—whether CWDM should retain the current fifth Department: Technical
Services for the remaining functions (Public Transport Regulation, Projects, Facilities
Management) following the roads function transfer.
1.6 The review must consider whether certain divisions from heavily burdened departments
should be repositioned under the Department: Technical Services to ensure balanced
workload distribution and functional coherence, consistent with functional structuring
principles (“form follows function”).
1.7 The Service Provider must review the structure of the Division: Internal Audit with the aim at
improving governance, reporting lines, career progression and compliance with the
International Professional Practices Framework (IPPF) and Global Internal Audit Standards
(GIAS 2025).

2. Section 4 (1) (b) (iii) of the Competition Act No. 89 of 1998, as amended, prohibits an
agreement between, or concerted practice by, firms, or a decision by an association of firms,
if it is between parties in a horizontal relationship and if it involves collusive bidding (or bid
rigging).² Collusive bidding is a pe se prohibition meaning that it cannot be justified under any
grounds.
3. Municipal Supply Regulation 38 (1) prescribes that a supply chain management policy must
provide measures for the combating of abuse of the supply chain management system, and
must enable the accounting officer, among others, to:
a. take all reasonable steps to prevent such abuse;
b. reject the bid of any bidder if that bidder or any of its directors has abused the supply
chain management system of the municipality or municipal entity or has committed any
improper conduct in relation to such system; and
c. cancel a contract awarded to a person if the person committed any corrupt or fraudulent
act during the bidding process or the execution of the contract.
4. This MBD serves as a certificate of declaration that would be used by institutions to ensure
that, when bids are considered, reasonable steps are taken to prevent any form of bid
rigging.
