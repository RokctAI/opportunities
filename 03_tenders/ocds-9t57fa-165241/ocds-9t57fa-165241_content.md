1. INTRODUCTION
Saldanha Bay Local Municipality (SBLM) supplement their water supply from groundwater
resources, especially during the dry summer months. Abstraction at the Langebaan Road
wellfield (both wellfield 1A and 1B), Hopefield wellfield and Hopefield Town Boreholes forms
part of the phased augmentation of water from groundwater supplies.
In 2019 an application was awarded to SBLM for a Water Use Licence (WUL) to utilise the
groundwater resource at the Langebaan Road 1A and 1B wellfields, with specific monitoring
requirements forming part of the WUL. An amendment WUL was issued in February 2025 to
correct the licenced volumes.
In 2023 an application was awarded to SBLM for a Water Use License (WUL) to utilise the
groundwater resource at Hopefield wellfield, with specific monitoring requirement forming
part of the WUL. An amendment application is currently in process.
A Water Use License (WUL) application is currently in process for the Hopefield Town
Boreholes.
The hydrogeological services required by SBLM include the monitoring and management of
the existing wellfields.
The tender period will commence on the date of the signing of the contract till 30 June 2029
to assist SBLM with:
• Specific WUL required groundwater monitoring at the Langebaan Road and
extended areas.
• Specific WUL required groundwater monitoring at the Hopefield wellfield and
extended areas.
• Specific WUL required groundwater monitoring at the Hopefield Town Boreholes and
extended areas.
• Calibrate the existing numerical aquifer and wellfield model for Langebaan Road and
Hopefield wellfields. The current model was completed in FeFlow.
• Provision of specialist inputs on development applications that involve utilisation of
groundwater or developments that could negatively impact on groundwater on an ad
hoc basis.
• Water pollution incident reporting on an ad hoc basis.
• Presenting monitoring results at the Langebaan Road, Hopefield wellfield and
Hopefield Town boreholes water monitoring committee meetings and providing

meetings.

3.1 Langebaan Road Aquifer (LRA) 3.1.1 Calibrate existing numerical aquifer and wellfield model for
and Hopefield (HPF) Wellfield Langebaan Road Aquifer and Hopefield (HPF) Wellfields (2
Combined Numerical Model calibrations during the contract period)
A numerical model was completed in FeFlow by December 2025 and
reviewed by June 2026. The existing FeFlow model needs to be
recalibrated twice during the contract period.
The objectives of the current numerical groundwater flow model include:
• Conceptual understanding of the regional aquifer based on
available data and reports;
• Establishment and calibration of a steady –and transient state
numerical model representing the aquifer systems at the project
site;
• Assessment of scenario-based predictions, thus reflecting longer-
term abstraction under different stress scenarios; and
• The results will guide future abstraction and provide
recommendations based on the simulations to optimise the
monitoring plan design.
To improve model reliability and reduce uncertainties, the following tasks
should form part of the broader recalibration of the model:
• Refined Spatial Discretisation: Consider reducing the model domain
around the wellfields to enable finer mesh resolution, particularly
around production and key monitoring boreholes, to better capture
local drawdown responses.
• Numerical Stability: Investigate and mitigate numerical instabilities
to allow shorter model run times. Consider applying the
unconfined/confined numerical formulation in FEFLOW.
77

• Automated Calibration: Transition from trial-and-error parameter
adjustments to automated calibration using PEST to optimise the
calibration.
• Incorporation of Private Abstraction Data: Include verified datasets
from the DWS once it is available to account for registered
abstractions in the wider region.
• Uncertainty Analysis: Conduct formal uncertainty analysis to
quantify model confidence.

Database Objectives:
1. Storage of Field Measurements: The database should be designed to
store time-stamped field measurements, including but not limited to
manual water level measurements, electrical conductivity measurements,
flowmeter readings, and field comments. This will ensure a centralised
and organised repository for easy data retrieval and analysis.
1. Borehole Attributes Storage: The database should allow for the storage
of relevant borehole attributes such as borehole depth, pump depth,
logger depth, critical water level, collar height, and borehole location. This
information will enable comprehensive analysis and understanding of the
hydrogeological properties associated with each borehole.
1. Data Integration: The database should facilitate the integration of data
from various sources, including water level loggers, telemetry systems,
rainfall tipping buckets, and chemistry analysis data from samples. This
data integration will provide a holistic view of the groundwater system and
its dynamics.

dashboard or similar application that allows users to view and interact with
the data. It should provide tables, charts and a spatial front end for easy
