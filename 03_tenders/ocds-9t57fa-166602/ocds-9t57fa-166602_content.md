8. PREFACE
1.1 Sivuyile Residential facility provides 24-hour residential care to persons (both adults and
children), with profound and severe intellectual disabilities. The facility provides care to an
extremely vulnerable group of people in society; a group of people that are not only
intellectually disabled but also has a number of other special conditions like, autism,
cerebral palsy, epilepsy, behavioural challenges, palliative care and some psychiatric
illnesses. The residents of the facility are in need of high care which imply that the home-
based carers are responsible to provide 100% care to the residents.

1. BID SPECIFICATIONS
3.1 Scope:
3.1.1 To provide an efficient and effective home-based care service at the Sivuyile Residential
Facility (situated at Stikland Hospital) for persons with severe and profound intellectual
and physical disabilities for a period of five (5) years. Currently, Sivuyile houses 110 such
residents. The service must be conducted in a spirit of compassion and understanding of
the needs of the residents.
3.2 Requirements
3.2.1 The requirements with regards to the number of home-based carers is stipulated in the
WCDB 3.2
3.3 Profile for home-based care workers:
3.3.1 Must be in possession of a basic SETA Accredited Home-Based Care qualification.
3.3.2 Each worker must be able to communicate (verbal and written) in at least two official
languages of the Western Cape, the total staff compliment must be able to converse in all
three official languages (English, Afrikaans, Xhosa) of the Western Cape.
3.3.3 Must be physically fit for purpose i.e. able to lift clients (children and adults), etc.
3.3.4 Must have previous experience in care for intellectual disabled/frail care and residential
care.
3.3.5 Must not have been declared unsuitable to work with children in terms of the Child Care
Act 38 of 2005 i.e. the person must not be listed in part B of the National Child Protection
Register.
3.3.6 Must not have a record of violence, neglect or abuse of persons.
3.4 Duties
3.4.1 Home-based care workers will be fully responsible to provide all the care needs. Their
duties will include but will not be limited to:
3.4.1.1 Full time supervision of all residents on a 24-hour basis.
3.4.1.2 Follow and strictly adhere to the structured daily program of the facility.
3.4.1.3 Physical and hygiene care to all residents including bathing, dressing, oral
hygiene, basic hair care and changing of nappies.
3.4.1.4 Feeding of residents including dishing of meals, feeding, feeding supplements,
tube feeding and preparation of milk supplements for tube feeding.
3.4.1.5 Assist with neatness of cupboards and cleanliness of assistive devices.
3.4.1.6 Assist cleaners with medical waste clearing from units.
3.4.1.7 Seating and positioning of residents according to prescribed assistive devices.
3.4.1.8 Turning of residents according to prescribed time frames.
3.4.1.9 Facilitate daily stimulation program, e.g arts & crafts, recreational sessions,
garden walks.
7

3.4.1.10 Facilitate and maintain a caring, therapeutic environment for residents.
3.4.1.11 Actively communicate with clients to assess their needs.
3.4.1.12 Participate in in-service training sessions.
3.4.1.13 Assist with self-help activities (potty training, brushing of teeth, etc.)
3.4.1.14 Manage input and output of residents.
3.4.1.15 Communicate with residents in an appropriate manner.
3.4.1.16 Report all reportable incidents in the prescribed manner.
3.4.1.17 Assist with folding and packing of washing.
3.4.1.18 Escort residents to hospital/community health centre appointments, family
visits, school attendance and/or protective workshops.
3.4.1.19 Provide written incident reports to nursing staff.
3.4.1.20 Document and report on:
(a) Behaviour of residents,
(b) Input and output,
(c) Personal hygiene,
(d) Daily occurrences,
(e) Stimulation program, and
(f) Reportable incidents. E.g, damage to property, injuries of residents,
health concerns.
3.4.2 Home-based care workers will perform their duties under the direct supervision of a
departmentally assigned representative which may either be a professional nurse,
enrolled nurse or an enrolled nursing assistant. Home-based care workers will be required
to collaborate with the nursing professional in planning the maintenance programme for
residents.
3.4.3 A detailed procedure manual will be provided for each post upon commencement of the
service.
3.4.4 All home-based care workers shall be required to complete the institution’s orientation
programme before services to residents can be rendered.
3.4.5 Any additional duty that are conferred upon the carer that relates to the operational
requirements for home-based care/residential care.
3.5 Conduct of home-based care workers
3.5.1 The code of conduct of the facility shall apply to the service provider and its staff.
3.5.2 The Provincial Government Western Cape expects the highest possible standards of
conduct from all staff. Transgressions that are non-compliant to the code of conduct is:
3.5.2.1 Absence without proper notification,
3.5.2.2 Accepting gifts or bribes in the line of duty,
3.5.2.3 Drinking intoxicating liquor or using drugs, being under its influence on duty or
reporting for duty while intoxicated,
3.5.2.4 Harshness or violence or using profane language,
8

3.5.2.5 Willful disobedience of site instructions, orders of a superior or a reasonable
request by a staff member or resident.
3.5.2.6 Failure to always wear the standard uniform when on duty.
3.5.2.7 Failure to complete occurrence book or any other documents as specified,
3.5.2.8 Failing to present an acceptable image, or to deal with any client in a
respectable manner,
3.5.2.9 Sleeping on duty, theft, eating food of clients, and withholding basic rights from
clients.
3.5.2.10 Zero tolerance on sexual harassment and related offences.
3.5.2.11 Any deviation in the Code of Conduct as prescribed by Sivuyile will be
regarded as extremely serious and may be sufficient reason to ask the service
provider to remove and replace the person permanently from the site.

1. SPECIAL CONDITIONS OF CONTRACT AND OPERATIONAL REQUIREMENTS
4.1 Specific operational requirements:
4.1.1 The service provider, upon commencement of the service, must provide proof of Hepatitis
B injections for all the staff that will be deployed at the site. The service provider must also
provide proof during the contract term of top-up of these injections.
4.1.2 The service provider shall not deploy persons that have been declared unsuitable to work
with children in terms of the Child Care Act 38 of 2005 i.e. the person must not be listed
in part B of the National Child Protection Register. Upon commencement of the service,
the service provider must provide proof of submission of form 29 as set out in the Child
Care Act 38 of 2005 for all the staff that will be deployed at the site. Thereafter to be
submitted annually.
4.1.3 The service provider, upon commencement of the service, must provide police clearances
for all the staff that will be deployed at the site. No person with a record of violence, abuse
or negligence to persons shall be deployed at the facility. Thereafter to be submitted
annually.
4.1.4 The Department’s authorised representative shall have the right to request the
replacement of any employee that does not comply with contract requirements.
4.1.5 The service provider must in consultation with the Department’s authorised representative
make replacement staff timeously available for induction and orientation purposes. No
home-based carer shall be allowed to provide care if such a person did not complete the
institution’s orientation programme.
4.1.6 All requirements stipulated in the bid document (special conditions, contract conditions,
specifications, etc.) where applicable, will remain for the duration of the contract term.
4.2 Entrance Control:
4.2.1 All carers will be subjected to control at the gate when entering the hospital premises.
4.2.2 Carers must identify themselves by recognized methods of identification before being
allowed on the premises. The service provider are responsible to provide the home-based
care worker with a company logo identification card, with the employee’s photo, full name,
and identification number.
4.2.3 The service provider and/or its staff will not be allowed to bring anything that is prohibited
(e.g. firearms, alcohol, etc.) onto the premises.
4.2.4 The service provider and/or its staff will not be allowed carry out any activity on the
premises that is prohibited by the Department.

unions or enquiries relating to labour related issues in respect of the Home-Based Care
Workers to take place at the facility during the working hours of the Home-Based Care
Workers, without the prior written consent of the Department;
4.3.2 Instruct Home Based Care Workers to duly complete the necessary documentation to
record the care status, including but not limited to, individual care plans, hygiene registers

4.3.3 Immediately repair, or replace, if necessary, any equipment and/ or goods of the

service. The training will not take longer than three days. The cost of the on-site orientation
