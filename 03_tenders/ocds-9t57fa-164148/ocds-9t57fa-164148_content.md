1. PURPOSE AND BACKROUND
1.1. The Department of Tourism, through the Working for Tourism Programme, funds and implements
the Tourism Monitors Programme, which is aligned to the Expanded Public Works Programme
(EPWP). The EPWP is a South African Government initiative aimed at drawing a significant number
of unemployed South Africans youths into productive work in a manner that enables them to gain
skills and increase their capacity to earn income as well as to contribute in developing their
communities and the country.
1.2. South Africa’s tourism industry has long grappled with safety challenges that impact its reputation
as a global destination. Incidents of crime directed at tourists and tourism products create a negative
perception and reputation about a destination and in turn impact on both the visitor experience and
visitor numbers. Crimes targeting tourists, such as hijackings, follow-on crimes, petty crimes and
extortions by imposters, remain key concerns.
1.3. To address these challenges, the Department of Tourism adopted a multifaceted Tourism Safety
Strategy. This strategy, implemented at both national and provincial levels, focuses on three
thematic areas: proactive measures, responsive measures, and aftercare programs. Proactive
measures aim to minimize risks by ensuring tourists feel secure even before their arrival, while
responsive measures enhance rapid response to incidents. The aftercare program provides support
to tourists affected by crimes.
1.4. The programme holds strategic importance as it not only supports the Tourism Safety Strategy but
also integrates broader socio-economic objectives. It aligns with national strategies such as job
creation, youth development, and community engagement, emphasising a collaborative approach
across the public and private sectors.
1.5. The Tourism Monitors Programme addresses safety concerns while tackling unemployment by
training and deploying unemployed youth at key tourism attractions. Its rationale lies in the dual need
to enhance safety and provide economic opportunities for the youth. By enhancing safety and
reducing crime, it helps restore confidence among international and domestic tourists, fostering
increased visitor numbers and improved experiences. The Programme equips participants with skills
and practical work experience, thus contributing to job creation, skills development and economic
development. Moreover, its role in community development strengthens social cohesion and local
economies, creating a virtuous cycle of growth and opportunity.
1.6. The Tourism Monitors Programme is aligned to Priority 1 of the Government of National Unity (GNU)
as outlined in the Medium-Term Development Plan (MTDP) of government; and is a key priority in
the new Strategic Plan and Annual Performance Plan (APP) of the Department.
1.7. The implementation of the programme, in various sites across the provinces, gets realised through
partnerships at national, provincial and local government levels. Therefore, the success of the
programme requires a collaborative approach with other stakeholders such as the provincial
departments responsible for Tourism, provincial Departments of Community Safety, Community
Policing Forums, local municipalities, other departments, Department of Home Affairs (DHA), Border
Management Agency, South African Police Services (SAPS), host employers, etc.
1.8. The Department has appointed Service Providers to implement the Tourism Safety Monitors
Programme nationally for a duration of twelve (12) months in four (4) provinces. Preparations are
underway to appoint service providers to manage the programme in five (5) Provinces namely
Eastern Cape, Northern Cape, North West Province, Limpopo and Mpumalanga. In consideration
of learners allocated per province for the remaining provinces, a cluster approach has been taken to
group learners for these provinces into three (3) regional clusters.
This approach will strengthen the administrative requirements and reduce project management
complexities on the team overseeing the implementation of the project.

1. OBJECTIVES OF THE PROGRAMME
The three main objectives of the Tourism Monitors Programme are to:
2.1 Enhance tourism safety awareness at key tourism attractions and sites;
2.2 Raise awareness and reduce the number of crime incidents that are directed at tourists who are
visiting provinces and the communities that host them; and
2.3 Provide skills and practical work experience to unemployed youth, utilising the vehicle of the
Expanded Public Works Programme (EPWP), to enable them to be employable.
