1. PURPOSE
1.1. To appoint a service provider that meets the pre-compliance, mandatory and functionality requirements
set out in this TOR, on a turnkey basis, to undertake the clean‑up, remediation and rehabilitation of the
Lichtenburg Waste Tyre Depot, following a fire incident.
1.2. The depot is located at Plot 15, Biesiesvlei, within the Ditsobotla Local Municipality, Northwest Province.
1.3. The service provider will be responsible for delivering a defined remediation outcome, namely the
restoration of the affected area to a safe, stable and environmentally compliant condition, in accordance
with applicable environmental legislation and standards, as further detailed in this TOR.
1. INTRODUCTION AND BACKGROUND
2.1 The Waste Management Bureau (WMB) (“the Bureau”) is established in terms of the National
Environmental Management: Waste Amendment Act, 2014 (Act No. 26 of 2014) and is responsible,
inter alia, for the promotion and facilitation of waste minimisation, re‑use, recycling and recovery of
waste in accordance with the National Environmental Management: Waste Act, 2008.
2.2 The Bureau’s primary focus includes the management of waste tyres, encompassing the collection of
waste tyres from dealers, temporary storage, and processing. In carrying out this mandate, the Bureau
leases land from the private sector for use as waste tyre depots and appoints operators to manage
these facilities. The Lichtenburg Waste Tyre Depot is one such leased site.
2.3 Circumstances necessitating remediation and rehabilitation of the Lichtenburg (Biesiesvlei) Waste Tyre
Depot:
2.3.1. The Lichtenburg Waste Tyre Depot is located on Plot 15, Biesiesvlei, approximately 40 km
from Lichtenburg in the Northwest Province, where the Bureau leases approximately 30,000
m² of land for the temporary storage and pre‑processing of waste tyres.
2.3.2. On 20 July 2023, a fire originating from adjacent properties, exacerbated by strong winds,
spread into the depot and impacted an estimated 28,500 m² of the site.
2.3.3. As a result of the fire, the site contains fire‑damaged waste tyre debris and ash, and may
include contaminated soil. These conditions necessitate remediation and subsequent
rehabilitation of the affected area. The nature and extent of any contamination will be
3

determined by the Service Provider through the required site investigations and sampling in
accordance with this TOR.
2.4 Debris, soil and ash within the southern portion of the site have been cleared and stockpiled northwards
in accordance with guidance provided by a specialist from the Department of Forestry, Fisheries and
the Environment (DFFE). This preparatory work enables the assessment, treatment and/or removal of
contaminated material under this contract.
2.5 The Bureau intends to appoint a service provider to undertake the remediation of fire‑affected material,
including the treatment, removal and/or disposal of waste tyre debris, soil and ash, as well as the
rehabilitation of the site, with the objective of restoring the affected area to a safe, stable and
environmentally compliant condition.
2.6 This procurement is structured as a turnkey, outcome‑based contract. While the Bureau does not
prescribe specific remediation methodologies, the appointed Service Provider shall be responsible for
ensuring that the final condition of the site complies with Soil Screening Values (SSV1), as prescribed
in the National Norms and Standards for the Remediation of Contaminated Land and Soil Quality,
promulgated under the National Environmental Management: Waste Act, 2008 (Act No. 59 of 2008).
1. OBJECTIVES
3.1. The primary objective of this project is to appoint a suitably qualified and experienced service provider,
on a turnkey basis, to undertake the clean‑up, remediation and rehabilitation of the site, such that the
affected area is restored to a safe, stable and environmentally compliant condition that is compatible
with its surrounding agricultural environment.
3.2. This includes, inter alia, the assessment, treatment, removal and/or disposal of fire‑affected waste tyre
debris, ash and contaminated soil, in a manner that ensures the final condition of the site complies with

Mandatory Analytical Scope and Minimum Investigation Requirements
Each bidder shall demonstrate that the pre-tender site investigation, undertaken at the bidder’s own cost,
included intrusive sampling and laboratory analysis sufficient to identify and characterise contaminants
associated with waste tyre storage and fire-affected residues, to the extent necessary to:

• Assess soil quality against the SSV1 Soil Screening Value prescribed in the National Norms and Standards for

and Standards for the Assessment of Waste for Landfill Disposal.
Minimum Investigation Baseline
Without limiting the bidder’s responsibility to undertake any additional investigation deemed necessary, the
pre-tender investigation shall include, as a minimum:
• A systematic intrusive sampling programme comprising test pits or equivalent, at a spacing not exceeding 50 m
across the site, supplemented by targeted sampling in visibly impacted areas (including burnt residues, ash
deposits, stained soils, and drainage pathways); and
• A minimum of 15 intrusive sampling locations distributed across the site.
Analytical Requirements
The analytical suite shall, as a minimum, include:
• Contaminants typically associated with waste tyre storage and fire-affected residues (including hydrocarbons
and combustion by-products);
• General soil quality parameters necessary to interpret contamination behaviour, mobility, and remediation
feasibility; and
• Any additional parameters deemed necessary by the bidder, based on site conditions and the proposed
remediation solution.
