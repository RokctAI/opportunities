1. BACKGROUND.
The National Film and Video Foundation (NFVF) is a public entity established in terms of the National
Film and Video Foundation Act, 1997 (Act No. 73 of 1997), and operates under the oversight of the
Department of Sport, Arts and Culture. The NFVF is mandated to promote and develop the South African
film and video industry by providing funding for film development, production, marketing, distribution, skills
development, research, and industry transformation initiatives. As part of its commitment to strengthening
organisational security, governance, and operational resilience, the NFVF seeks to appoint a suitably
qualified and experienced service provider to design, supply, install, configure, commission, support, and
maintain a modern biometric access control system for its offices located at 87 Central Street, Houghton
Estate, Johannesburg.
The proposed solution will provide secure, centrally managed access to designated areas within the
NFVF offices, including the ICT server room, through biometric and card-based authentication. The
system is expected to improve physical security, enhance accountability through comprehensive audit
trails, strengthen the protection of organisational assets and information, and support compliance with
applicable governance, information security, and data protection requirements.
The successful bidder will be responsible for delivering a fully operational solution, including all required
hardware, software, licensing, installation, configuration, testing, documentation, training, warranty, and
maintenance services for a period of three (3) years.
1. BACKGROUND AND SERVICE REQUIRED
The NFVF occupies the first floor of its offices at 87 Central Street, Houghton Estate, Johannesburg.
Access to the office environment is currently controlled by an ageing biometric access control system that
has reached the end of its useful life. The existing solution has become unreliable, is no longer
adequately supported, and does not fully meet the organisation's current operational, security, and
governance requirements. The current environment comprises multiple controlled access points, including
the main office entrances, the basement access point, and the ICT server room. Due to the limitations of
the existing system, the NFVF requires a modern, centrally managed access control solution capable of
securing these areas while providing improved reliability, scalability, and ease of administration.
The new system will enhance physical security by ensuring that only authorised personnel are granted
access to restricted areas through biometric and card-based authentication. The solution must also
provide comprehensive audit trails, centralised administration, reporting capabilities, backup power, and
secure storage of access records to support operational oversight and audit requirements.

The successful service provider will be responsible for supplying, installing, configuring, testing,
commissioning, documenting, supporting, and maintaining a complete biometric access control solution
that meets the NFVF's current operational needs while allowing for future expansion.
1. BUSINESS NEED
The NFVF requires a modern biometric access control system to strengthen physical security, improve
access management, and safeguard organisational assets, personnel, and information. The existing
access control solution no longer adequately supports the organisation's operational and security
requirements and requires replacement with a secure, reliable, and centrally managed solution. The
implementation of a new biometric access control system will enable the NFVF to:
• Restrict access to authorised personnel only through biometric and RFID card authentication.
• Secure critical areas, including office entrances, the basement access point, and the ICT server
room.
• Provide centralised management of users, access permissions, and system configuration.
• Maintain comprehensive audit trails of all access events for monitoring, reporting, investigations,
and audit purposes.
• Improve accountability by recording all entry attempts, successful access events, and denied
access events.
• Enhance business continuity through backup power and resilient system design.
• Strengthen compliance with the Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA), applicable
information security standards, and internal governance requirements.
• Support future organisational growth by providing a scalable solution capable of accommodating
additional users, access points, and security enhancements.
The proposed solution will form a key component of the NFVF's physical security strategy and will
contribute towards improving organisational governance, reducing the risk of unauthorised access, and
protecting critical ICT infrastructure and information assets.
1. OBJECTIVES
The primary objectives of this procurement are to:
4.1. Strengthen Physical Security
Implement a modern biometric access control system that restricts access to authorised personnel
and protects the NFVF's offices, employees, visitors, ICT infrastructure, and other critical assets.
4.2. Centralise Access Management
Provide a centrally managed platform for administering users, access rights, schedules, and
security policies across all controlled access points.

4.3. Improve Accountability and Auditability
Maintain comprehensive audit logs and reporting capabilities to support operational monitoring,
investigations, internal controls, and audit requirements.
4.4. Enhance Operational Efficiency
Simplify the enrolment, management, and removal of users while reducing reliance on manual
access control processes.
4.5. Improve Reliability and Business Continuity
Provide a resilient access control solution incorporating backup power and appropriate fail-safe
mechanisms to ensure continuous operation during power interruptions and system failures.
4.6. Protect Critical ICT Infrastructure
Provide enhanced physical protection for the ICT server room and other restricted areas through
controlled biometric and card-based access.
4.7. Ensure Compliance
Support compliance with the Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA), applicable information
security standards, and the NFVF's internal governance, risk management, and security policies.
4.8. Provide a Scalable Solution
Implement a solution that can be expanded in future to accommodate additional users, access
points, authentication methods, and integration with other security technologies as the
organisation's requirements evolve.

1. FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS
The proposed biometric access control solution shall provide the following minimum functional
capabilities:
7.1. Authentication
The solution shall support:
• Fingerprint authentication.
• RFID card authentication.
• The ability to use either biometric authentication, RFID card authentication, or both, as configured
by the NFVF.
• Rapid user authentication with minimal delay.

7.2. Access Control
The solution shall:
• Restrict access to authorised personnel only.
• Support configurable access rights based on users, departments, or groups.
• Allow the configuration of access schedules, working hours, weekends, and public holidays.
• Support immediate activation and deactivation of user access.
7.3. User Management
The solution shall provide:
• User enrolment, modification, suspension, and deletion.
• Role-based administration.
• Bulk import and export of users where applicable.
• User profile management.
7.4. Audit Trails and Reporting
The solution shall:
• Record all successful and unsuccessful access attempts.
• Record date, time, user identity, authentication method, and door accessed.
• Generate audit reports for security monitoring and investigations.
• Allow reports to be exported in commonly used formats (e.g., PDF and Microsoft Excel).
7.5. Administration
The solution shall provide:
• Centralised management of all access points.
• Local and remote administration capabilities.
• Secure administrator authentication.
• Configuration backup and restore functionality.
7.6. Notifications and Alerts
The solution should provide alerts for:
• Forced door openings.
• Door held open events.
• Communication failures.
• Power failures.
• Hardware faults.

7.7. Scalability
The solution shall:
• Support future expansion to additional doors, users, and authentication devices.
• Allow additional biometric readers and controllers to be incorporated without replacing the existing
system.
7.8. Availability
The solution shall provide high system availability with minimal downtime and shall automatically
resume normal operation following power restoration or system recovery.
