SECTION 3
BACKGROUND, OVERVIEW AND SCOPE OF REQUIREMENTS
REQUEST FOR PROPOSAL (RFP): FOR THE APPOINTMENT OF A SERVICE PROVIDER TO
IMPLEMENT EXECUTIVE TEAM COACHING AND INDIVIDUAL EXECUTIVE COACHING
SERVICES FOR A PERIOD OF NOT LONGER THAN 18 MONTHS.
1. INTRODUCTION AND BACKGROUND
1.1. SANRAL was established in April 1998 by an Act of Parliament as an independent statutory
company operating along commercial lines. The purpose of the organisation, which is registered in
terms of the Companies Act – with the Minister of Transport as the sole shareholder – is to maintain
and develop South Africa’s expanding national road network.
SANRAL is a knowledge-based organisation which is engaged in national programme management
execution as a client body within the public sector infrastructure development environment. SANRAL
requires leaders who are resilient, tactical, agile, and well equipped to respond to the ever-changing
environment under which the organisation operates. Some of the challenges affecting this environment
include:
• Increasing demand to implement infrastructure development programmes which address socio-
economic challenges and make an impact.
• Change – local and global - remaining relevant in the ever-changing nature of innovation and
business.
• Scarcity of high-level skills in project and programme management related to civil engineering and
related professions and the management thereof.
• Maintaining financial sustainability.
• Managing in a turbulent environment.
To enhance leadership effectiveness, foster a high-performance culture, improve strategic alignment,
and strengthen collaborative decision-making, SANRAL seeks to procure the services of a qualified and
experienced service provider to deliver comprehensive executive team coaching and individual
executive coaching for its executive level leaders.
This intervention is aligned with SANRAL’s commitment to continuous improvement, good governance,
and the development of its human capital to better serve the citizens of South Africa.
The coaching programme is expected to address current leadership challenges, build on existing
strengths, and equip executives with the necessary skills and mindset to navigate complex
environments and achieve SANRAL’s strategic objectives.
8 | Page

1. PROJECT GOAL AND OBJECTIVES
2.1. Overall Goal: To enhance the individual and collective leadership capabilities of the SANRAL
executive team, leading to improved organisational performance, strategic foresight and execution,
and a more cohesive and effective leadership cohort.
2.2. Specific Objectives:
2.2.1. For Executive Team Coaching:
a. To improve team cohesion, trust, and psychological safety within the executive team.
b. To ensure that the team understands each other’s strengths and weaknesses, leadership styles
c. To establish the leadership team’s collective personality profile, and its impact on delivery of
objectives
d. To enhance strategic alignment and shared understanding of organisational goals and priorities.
e. To strengthen collective problem-solving, decision-making, and accountability.
f. To improve inter-departmental collaboration and communication.
g. To foster a culture of constructive feedback and continuous learning within the team.
h. To equip the team to lead and manage organisational change effectively.
2.2.2. For Individual Executive Coaching:
a. To enhance self-awareness and understanding of individual leadership strengths and development
areas.
b. To develop critical leadership competencies identified through assessments or performance feedback
(e.g., strategic thinking, communication, emotional intelligence, conflict resolution, change leadership).
c. To support executives in achieving their individual performance goals and professional development
aspirations.
d. To improve personal effectiveness, resilience, and well-being.
e. To provide a confidential sound board for executives to address specific leadership challenges.
f. To enhance stakeholder acumen allowing for navigation of complex stakeholder environments,
including political-administrative interfaces, oversight structures, and community engagement.

1. SCOPE OF WORK
The selected service provider will be required to design, develop, and deliver a tailored executive
coaching programme that includes both team and individual coaching components.
3.1. Phase 1: Needs Assessment, Programme Design, and Contracting
3.1.1. Conduct a thorough needs assessment through consultations with key stakeholders (e.g.,
Accounting Officer/CEO, CHRO, executive team members) and review of relevant SANRAL documents
(e.g., strategic plan, performance reports, previous staff surveys).
3.1.2. Develop a comprehensive and customised executive coaching programme proposal based on
the needs assessment, outlining the methodology, coaching models, tools, timelines, and expected
outcomes for both team and individual coaching.
3.1.3. Present the proposed programme to the SANRAL for review and approval.
3.1.4. Establish clear coaching agreements/contracts with individual executives and the executive team,
outlining roles, responsibilities, confidentiality, and coaching objectives. This includes a three-way
contract meeting between the coach, coachee, and their line manager (or a designated SANRAL
representative, e.g., CHRO) for individual coaching to align goals.
3.1.5 Develop a clear set of success indicators to be used in Phase 4: monitoring, reporting and
evaluation
3.2. Phase 2: Executive Team Coaching
3.2.1. Design and facilitate a series of team coaching sessions/workshops for the executive team.
3.2.2. The coaching should focus on the objectives outlined in section 2.2.1.
3.2.3. Utilize appropriate team diagnostic tools and assessment methodologies (e.g., team
effectiveness surveys, psychometric assessments for teams – to be agreed upon with the SANRAL).
3.2.4. Facilitate the development of a team charter or action plan to embed learning and track progress.
3.2.5. Provide ongoing support and resources to the team as needed between sessions.
3.2.6. The anticipated number of team coaching days/sessions is [Specify number, e.g., 6-8 full-day
sessions or equivalent] over a period of [Specify duration, e.g., 12 months].
3.3. Phase 3: Individual Executive Coaching
3.3.1. Provide one-on-one coaching sessions for designated executives.
3.3.2. The coaching should focus on the objectives outlined in section 2.2.2 and individual development
plans.
3.3.3. Utilize appropriate individual assessment tools (e.g., 360-degree feedback, psychometric
assessments – to be agreed upon with the SANRAL and individual coachees).
10 | Page

3.3.4. Support executives in creating and implementing individual leadership development plans.
3.3.5. Each designated executive is expected to receive approximately [Specify number, e.g., 10-12
hours] of individual coaching, delivered over [Specify duration, e.g., 6-12 months]. Sessions are typically
1.5 to 2 hours in duration.
3.4. Phase 4: Monitoring, Evaluation, and Reporting
3.4.1. Implement a system for monitoring the progress and effectiveness of the coaching interventions,
using the success indicators developed in Phase 1.
3.4.2. Conduct regular check-in meetings with SANRAL designated project manager/HR representative.
3.4.3. Provide interim progress reports and a final comprehensive report detailing activities undertaken,
outcomes achieved against objectives, challenges encountered, lessons learned, and
recommendations for sustained leadership development.
3.4.4. Conduct a post-programme evaluation to assess the impact of the coaching (e.g., through
surveys, interviews).
1. DELIVERABLES
The service provider will be expected to deliver the following:
4.1. A detailed Inception Report, including the needs assessment findings, finalized coaching
programme design (team and individual), methodologies, tools, and detailed implementation plan with
timelines.
4.2. Coaching agreements/contracts for each individual coachee and the executive team.
4.3. All materials, workbooks, and resources required for team coaching sessions and individual
coaching.
4.4. Facilitation of all agreed-upon team coaching sessions/workshops.
4.5. Delivery of all agreed-upon individual executive coaching hours.
4.6. Individual Leadership Development Plans for each coachee.
4.7. Risk management plan
4.8 Post Coaching support plan
4.9 Interim progress reports (e.g., quarterly, or as agreed).
4.10. A final comprehensive report at the conclusion of the coaching programme, including an overall
assessment of the programme's impact, anonymous themed insights (while maintaining individual
confidentiality), and recommendations.
4.11. A presentation of the final report and findings to SANRAL leadership.
4.12. The coaching will follow a blended engagement approach. All team engagement sessions will be
in person. Individual coaching sessions may be in person (90%) or online (10%).

Fronting Practices
Window-dressing: This includes cases in which black people are appointed or introduced to an
enterprise on the basis of tokenism and may be:
• Discouraged or inhibited from substantially participating in the core activities of an enterprise;
and
• Discouraged or inhibited from substantially participating in the stated areas and/or levels of their

Benefit Diversion: This includes initiatives implemented where the economic benefits received as a
result of the B-BBEE Status of an enterprise do not flow to black people in the ratio as specified in the
relevant legal documentation.
Opportunistic Intermediaries: This includes enterprises that have concluded agreements with other
enterprises with a view to leveraging the opportunistic intermediary's favourable B-BBEE status in
circumstances where the agreement involves:
• Significant limitations or restrictions upon the identity of the opportunistic intermediary's suppliers,
Service Providers, clients or customers;
• The maintenance of their business operations in a context reasonably considered improbable
having regard to resources; and
• Terms and conditions that are not negotiated at arms-length on a fair and reasonable basis.
Responsibility to Report Fronting
In order to effectively deal with the scourge of Fronting, verification agencies, and/or procurement
officers and relevant decision makers are encouraged to obtain a signed declaration from the clients or
entities that they verify or provide business opportunities to, which states that the client or entity
understands and accepts that the verification agency, procurement officer or relevant decision maker
may report Fronting practices to the dtic. Intentional misrepresentation by measured entities may
constitute fraudulent practices, public officials and verification agencies are to report such cases to the
dtic.
Fronting Indicators
• The black people identified by an enterprise as its shareholders, executives or management are
unaware or uncertain of their role within an enterprise;
• The black people identified by an enterprise as its shareholders, executives or management
have roles of responsibility that differ significantly from those of their non-black peers;
• The black people who serve in executive or management positions in an enterprise are paid
significantly lower than the market norm, unless all executives or management of an enterprise
are paid at a similar level;
• There is no significant indication of active participation by black people identified as top manage-
ment at strategic decision making level;
• An enterprise only conducts peripheral functions and does not perform the core functions
reasonably expected of other, similar, enterprises;
• An enterprise relies on a third-party to conduct most core functions normally conducted by
enterprises similar to it;

related person or shareholder;
• An enterprise obtains loans, not linked to the good faith share purchases or enterprise develop-

a. “tender” means a written offer in the form determined by an organ of state in response
to an invitation to provide goods or services through price quotations, competitive tendering
process or any other method envisaged in legislation;
b. “price” means an amount of money tendered for goods or services, and includes all
applicable taxes less all unconditional discounts;
c. “rand value” means the total estimated value of a contract in Rand, calculated at the time
of bid invitation, and includes all applicable taxes;
d. “tender for income-generating contracts” means a written offer in the form determined
by an organ of state in response to an invitation for the origination of income-generating
contracts through any method envisaged in legislation that will result in a legal agreement
between the organ of state and a third party that produces revenue for the organ of state,
and includes, but is not limited to, leasing and disposal of assets and concession contracts,
excluding direct sales and disposal of assets through public auctions; and
e. “the Act” means the Preferential Procurement Policy Framework Act, 2000 (Act No. 5 of
2000).
