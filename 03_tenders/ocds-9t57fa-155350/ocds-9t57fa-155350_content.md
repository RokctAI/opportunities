1. INTRODUCTION TO SARAO
The South African Radio Astronomy Observatory (SARAO, formerly in part SKA SA) is the
business unit of the National Research Foundation (NRF) that houses and operates South
Africa’s national radio telescope shared facilities, including the MeerKAT, KAT-7, and
Hartebeesthoek 26-m radio telescopes.
As a member of the international effort to build the Square Kilometre Array (SKA), which will
be the world’s largest radio telescope, South Africa (through the NRF’s business units) is
responsible for the construction and operation of the MeerKAT precursor telescope, for hosting
the SKA Mid Frequency telescope, and for implementation of the African VLBI Network.
The SARAO project office has offices based in Johannesburg and Cape Town, as well as the
radio-quiet SKA host site in the Karoo, 90km from Carnarvon in the Northern Cape, which
hosts the Square Kilometre Array mid-frequency telescopes, MeerKAT, and KAT-7 radio
telescope installations, as well as a number of guest instruments, including the HERA
telescope.
SARAO requires the appointment of a legally registered and authorised service provider for
the integrated management of hazardous waste and healthcare risk waste (HCRW), including
collection, transportation, treatment, and disposal in compliance with applicable South African
legislation.
