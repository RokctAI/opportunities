TERMS OF REFERENCE
1. INTRODUCTION
SAFCOL is a state-owned forestry company listed as a Schedule 2 major public entity in terms of the
Public Finance Management Act 1 of 1999. It was established in 1992 following the promulgation of the
Management of State Forest Act 128 of 1992 to promote the development in the long term of the forestry
industry according to accepted commercial management practice in South Africa. Through its wholly
owned subsidiary, Komatiland Forests (KLF), SAFCOL conducts its forestry operations and manages
plantation assets in various regions of the country.
The South African Forestry Company SOC Limited (SAFCOL) is seeking information from the marketplace
regarding potential solutions, technologies, products, services, and industry capabilities relating to steep
area extraction solutions for forestry harvesting and thinning operations:
The purpose of this Request for Information (RFI) is to gather market intelligence to identify suitable steep
area extraction solutions for forestry harvesting clearfell and thinning operations and possible
procurement activities.
1. BACKGROUND
The Tactical Harvesting Plan identifies the compartments scheduled for harvesting and thinning over the
next five-year period. It determines the planned volumes, timing, location, terrain conditions, tree size
information, and operational requirements needed to select appropriate harvesting and extraction
systems. The plan is used to guide resource planning, contractor requirements, equipment selection,
procurement decisions, safety planning, and the implementation of suitable solutions for both conventional
and difficult steep area terrain operations.

KZN. Table 1 below displays the relevant plantations that may utilise difficult steep area terrain

2.4 Strategic objectives
SAFCOL’s strategic direction places strong emphasis on business growth, customer centricity,
excellence in forestry operations, a future-fit organisation, financial sustainability and responsible
forest management. The RFI for difficult steep area extraction solutions directly supports these
strategic goals by enabling SAFCOL to identify practical, safe, and commercially viable technologies
that can improve harvesting productivity in areas where conventional systems are limited.
The RFI will assist SAFCOL to advance operational excellence by exploring mechanised and
technology-enabled solutions that can improve asset productivity, increase harvesting efficiency,
reduce operational bottlenecks, and strengthen the company’s ability to meet planned harvesting
volumes. It also supports employee safety by reducing exposure to high-risk manual or motor-manual
operations in difficult terrain, while promoting safer, more controlled extraction methods.
In addition, the RFI contributes to financial sustainability and business growth by identifying solutions
that may reduce dependency on limited specialist contractors, improve cost predictability, support
better utilisation of forestry assets, and strengthen SAFCOL’s ability to supply customers consistently.
By requesting information on local support, maintenance, spares, training, pricing, and total cost of
ownership, the RFI will also help inform future procurement decisions and ensure that potential
solutions are practical to implement and sustainable over the long term.
The initiative further supports SAFCOL’s future-fit and innovation objectives by encouraging the
evaluation of modern steep terrain technologies, including excavator-based shovel and grapple
yarders, cable yarding systems, winch-assisted harvesting, tethered machines, and skyline systems.
These solutions align with SAFCOL’s broader focus on technology adoption, research, operational
improvement, and responsible forest management, while also creating opportunities for industry
benchmarking, supplier development, skills transfer, and improved environmental performance
The phased mechanisation expansion project initiated in 2023 provides SAFCOL with a clear
opportunity to improve operational safety, enhance harvesting efficiency, and reduce dependence on
higher-risk manual and motor-manual methods. Accordingly, it is important that suitable solutions for
difficult steep area terrain are actively investigated to support the continued expansion of
mechanisation within SAFCOL’s harvesting strategy.
